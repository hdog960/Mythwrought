# Authority 02 — Story, geography and exploration
**Status:** target narrative/world design; specifics remain to author.

## Core premise
The world's magic is failing. Surface kingdoms and deep civilizations compete for declining magical resources while ancient sleeping buried beings underlie the origin and possible cost of civilization's magical energy. Knowledge, nature, extraction, political survival and buried historical wrongdoing intersect. No faction is universally righteous or evil.

## Four broad movements
1. **Local signs:** Starting valley's aging magical water infrastructure fails; local engineer, naturalist, scholar and miner disagree on causes and remedy. Player can help by real-world mechanical/environmental means or delay main quest. Background affects introduction.
2. **Contested resources:** Surface and subterranean factions dispute trade, extraction, conservation and sovereignty; regional choices alter relationships and local infrastructure.
3. **Beneath the histories:** Deeper settlements, ancient ruins and conflicting records reveal large-scale magical exploitation and truths about sleeping beings.
4. **Meaningful resolution:** Multiple ethically defensible approaches to the world's future; consequences for key factions and important characters. No binary morality slider; do not assume choices require massive bespoke cinematics.

## Opening (first 30 minutes, player-paced)
Hero arrives as a TRAVELER with background-dependent motive (soldier, scholar, outlander, artisan, criminal, acolyte, merchant, miner, potentially more). Start on a pleasant hillside above a seeded but authored-adapted valley settlement. Immediate agency: talk to residents, wander, harvest, craft/place blocks, learn basic combat/magic, enter an ordinary cave. See a failing runic water system, fungal chamber and a distant glimpse of immense inhabited caverns below. Avoid mandatory long cutscene, forced first battle, forced building tutorial or arbitrary underground barrier.

## World placement invariant
Seed determines physical geography, biome, depth, ores and minor ruins. Required major authored sites/cities are deterministic overlays that select geologically sensible placements, reserve geometry, generate reachable entrances/roads, resolve collision with other authored sites and satisfy quest dependencies. Site placement validation occurs before seed is considered supported. Quest scripts treat excavated shortcuts, alternate entrances, broken devices and changes to terrain as possible states.

## Surface
Warm varied biomes, dense forests, villages, roads, rivers, mountains, lakes, seasonal/regional farming choices, mining, ruins, wildlife and user-created settlements. Bright high fantasy with deep politics and pockets of corruption. Build anywhere reasonably editable and safe; no mandatory property plots.

## Underground
One continuous volume below surface. Minecraft-style shallow caves and resource clusters; immense multilevel caverns inspired by the sense of scale of TOTK's Depths; BG3-like distinct societies, economy, secrets and ecology. Three visual conditions intermix: (1) vast near-black regions, (2) wondrous luminous mushroom/crystal/water ecosystems, (3) warm inhabited cities. Dense NETWORK of stone kingdoms, goblin freeholds, fungal settlements, deepwater communities, forgotten enclaves, trading hubs, highways and hidden civilizations; vast wild expanses and danger remain between. Some cities trade openly with the surface; others are isolated or forgotten. Culture is not biologically moral. The underground can be home, not only endgame ore harvesting.

## World-scale visual composition (concept discussion, 2026-11-11)
The user's aspirational images emphasize **whimsical, colorful wonder with enormous sense of scale**. Translate this into original editable voxel geography: warm markets and villages in sight of dramatic bridges, distant castles, waterfalls, flowering woodland, winter routes, layered harbors and mysterious ancient ruins. Meaningful distant landmarks should help the player navigate and want to explore; they are not promises of identical geometry or of vast cities immediately appearing in the prototype.

The same tonal range extends underground: extraordinary bioluminescent habitats and warmly inhabited stone cities deserve coherent skylines, bridges, water and local life, contrasted against truly dark, forbidding uninhabited reaches. Monumental or terrifying threats can emerge from this inviting world without making everyday life uniformly grim. Use the art and UX authority's [whimsical high-fantasy notes](05_ART_AND_UX.md) as the visual guidance. All major authored sites still follow the existing seeded placement, accessibility and editability invariants. This is **design direction only**, not implemented content or an approved art gate.

## Traversal and transport
Player walks, digs, builds bridges and staircases, uses spells, elevators, minecart routes and ordinary world paths. No load screen between cave/city/surface. Recognizable player map/compass markers and custom pins. World atlas offers surface, cavern and deep layers; unknown sites hidden. Place personal named/colored/icon portal ANCHORS in valid spots; world-map itself selects fast-travel destination; cast temporary horizontal dimensional opening and jump into it, arriving safely with two active companions. Anchors are separate from map pins and runic relay crystals. Ordinary travel magic is safe. Destinations never fabricated.

## Life beyond quests
Optional minor villages/camps, trade caravans, NPC routines and factions in both strata. Regional event and economy effects stay bounded, deterministic and explainable. Player-built routes can enable trade when passable and permissions/agreements exist, not when two raw tunnels merely touch.

## Narrative resilience
Quest progress not tied to one fragile destructible door/lever. Reserve essential NPC fallback or relocation behavior. Important secrets are discoverable by more than one consistent clue where possible. Do not let procedural generation accidentally create impossible main-story states.

## Writing quality gate
Deep friendship/found family, banter, conflicting personal values and meaningful consequences; no romance. Village side stories need not all connect to gods. The main plot remains discoverable through exploration and dialogue, not giant exposition dumps.
